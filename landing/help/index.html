Getting started

What is Aegis Browser?

A Chromium-based browser hardened for privacy: ads and trackers blocked at the network level, telemetry off, no Google services running by default.

Getting help & feedback

How to reach this documentation from the browser menu, settings, or keyboard help entries.

Checking your version

Open Menu → Settings → About Aegis (or chrome://version) to see the exact Chromium version and Aegis release number.

Privacy & Shield

How Shield works

Shield blocks ads and trackers before they load: 92,957+ known tracking hosts are refused at the network level. Main-frame navigation is never blocked — you can always reach the site itself.

The Shield count

The number you see in the UI is how many third-party requests were blocked on the pages you visited during this session.

Cookies

Third-party cookies are blocked by default. First-party cookies work normally so sites stay signed in.

Incognito

Incognito works as you expect: nothing from the session is written to history, cookies or storage after you close the last Incognito tab.

Safe Browsing protection

Local phishing/malware heuristics stay on. Network lookups for enhanced protection are off by default — Aegis sends no browsing data anywhere.

Telemetry

Usage statistics and crash reports are off by default and cannot be turned on through settings prompts. “Do Not Track” is sent by default.

Sync & Aegis ID

Aegis ID sign-in

Aegis ID is your account for Aegis Sync — created inside Aegis, not with Google. Sign in from Settings → Aegis ecosystem → Aegis ID.

How sync encryption works

Your data is end-to-end encrypted on your device. The sync server stores only opaque ciphertext — it never sees your passwords or vault contents.

Managing devices

Each signed-in device is listed under your Aegis ID. You can revoke any device at any time; it immediately loses access to new sync data.

Features

Passwords

Aegis saves passwords locally on your device. There is no Google account in the loop — nothing is uploaded unless you turn on Aegis Sync with your Aegis ID.

Autofill

Addresses and payment methods are stored locally. They are offered only to sites you submit forms on.

Installing web apps

Use Menu → Add to Home screen (Android) or the install icon in the omnibox (desktop) to install a site as an app.

Device connections

Cast, Bluetooth, USB and serial device prompts work as in Chromium; nothing connects without your explicit action.

Search engines

Google is the default search engine out of the box. You can change it anytime in Settings → Search engine; suggestions are off by default.

AI assistants

AI features are not built into Aegis. If you want an AI service, just open its website (for example gemini.google.com) — nothing runs inside the browser by default.

Troubleshooting

A site looks broken

Some sites misbehave when trackers are blocked. Try chrome://settings/shield to review Shield, or test in Incognito. If it persists, report the site via feedback.

Sign-in problems

Websites sign in normally. Google account sign-in inside browser UI is disabled by design — Google services work as websites, not as browser-embedded services.

Performance

Aegis blocks most trackers before they run, so pages usually load faster. If a page is slow, check Shield’s blocked count — heavy pages often hide dozens of trackers.

Certificate warnings

Certificate warnings are shown exactly as Chromium presents them. Never proceed past one unless you are certain about the site.
